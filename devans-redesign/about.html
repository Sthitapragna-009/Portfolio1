Since 1962

Sixty-plus years, one market stall.

Devan's began as a single coffee counter in Khanna Market, Lodhi Colony — and has stayed there through three generations of the same family.

Coffee roasting in India runs through a handful of families, and Devan's is one of them. What started as a small counter selling filter coffee to the neighbourhood in 1962 grew, slowly and mostly by word of mouth, into a roastery sourcing green Arabica directly from estates in Chikmagalur, Coorg and the Malabar coast.

The roasting hasn't moved far from where it started, and neither has the approach: small batches, roasted to order rather than stockpiled, and blends built by hand-mixing beans roasted at different stages rather than by formula. The Viennese, Lodhi and Oriental Espresso blends on this site are all originals developed in-house.

1962

A counter in Khanna Market

Devan's opens as a single coffee and tea counter in Lodhi Colony, New Delhi, selling fresh-roasted filter coffee to a neighbourhood clientele.

1970s–1990s

Sourcing direct from the hills

Relationships are built directly with Arabica estates in Chikmagalur and Coorg, and with the monsoon-curing warehouses on the Malabar coast.

2000s

The house blends take shape

The Lodhi, Viennese and Oriental Espresso blends are developed and refined over hundreds of small batches, each named for either the place or the people behind it.

Today

Third generation, same market

Still family-run out of Khanna Market, now roasting single origins, house blends, cold brew bags and coffee for wholesale cafe accounts across India.

The Future of Coffee

Printed on every Devan's bag: a coffee-strength gauge sits next to a conservation status for an animal species — from vulnerable to critically endangered.

Why It's There

At least 60% of wild coffee species are threatened with extinction as heat, drought and shifting rainfall reshape the estates we roast from — the same climate pressure behind species loss.

How we choose beans

Every coffee is graded at origin before it ever reaches Delhi.
